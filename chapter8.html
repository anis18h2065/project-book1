Unit 8: Information Security and Cyber Law

1. Introduction to Information Security

Information Security (InfoSec) refers to the practices, policies, and technologies designed to protect digital data, computer systems, and networks from unauthorized access, disclosure, disruption, modification, or destruction.

2. Core Pillars: CIA Triad

The foundation of information security rests on three primary objectives known as the CIA Triad:

  • Confidentiality: Ensuring that sensitive data is accessible only to authorized personnel and protected from unauthorized viewing (e.g., using Encryption and Passwords).
  • Integrity: Safeguarding the accuracy and completeness of data by preventing unauthorized alterations or tampering (e.g., using Hash functions and Digital Signatures).
  • Availability: Ensuring that authorized users have reliable and timely access to information systems whenever needed (e.g., using Hardware Backups and Redundant Servers).

3. Common Security Threats and Vulnerabilities

Digital systems face numerous threats categorized under malicious activity, user errors, or structural defects:

A. Malicious Software (Malware)

Malware Type Description
Virus A self-replicating executable program that attaches itself to legitimate clean files and spreads across systems when executed.
Worm A standalone malicious program that automatically replicates and spreads through computer networks without human interaction.
Trojan Horse A malicious code disguised as legitimate software to trick users into installing it, creating backdoors for attackers.
Ransomware Encrypts user files or locks systems, demanding a monetary ransom to restore access.
Spyware Secretly monitors user activities, keystrokes, and personal credentials without user knowledge.

B. Network and Social Engineering Attacks

  • Phishing: Fraudulent emails or websites mimicking trusted organizations to trick users into revealing passwords or credit card numbers.
  • Denial of Service (DoS / DDoS): Flooding a targeted server or network with fake traffic to render it completely inaccessible to legitimate users.
  • Man-in-the-Middle (MitM): An attacker secretly intercepts and alters communication between two trusting parties.
  • Hacking: Gaining unauthorized entry into remote computer systems or network infrastructures.

4. Security Measures and Prevention Techniques

Organizations and individuals implement multiple security controls to mitigate cyber risks:

  • Antivirus & Anti-Malware Software: Programs designed to detect, isolate, and remove malicious code from system storage.
  • Firewall: A network security system (hardware or software) that monitors and filters incoming and outgoing network traffic based on predefined security rules.
  • Data Cryptography: Encoding plain text into unreadable ciphertext using mathematical keys to secure data transmission.
  • Authentication & Authorization: Verifying user identity using Multi-Factor Authentication (MFA), strong passwords, and biometric scans.
  • Data Backup: Maintaining periodic offsite or cloud backups to recover data in case of disaster or system failure.

5. Cyber Ethics & Cyber Crime

Cyber Ethics refers to the moral guidelines and codes of conduct governing the legal and respectful use of internet resources and digital technologies.

Cyber Crime refers to any illegal act committed using computers, digital devices, or the internet as a tool or target. Common examples include identity theft, software piracy, cyberbullying, financial fraud, and unauthorized hacking.

6. Intellectual Property Right (IPR)

Intellectual Property Rights protect legal rights granted to individuals over the creations of their minds (inventions, literary works, software, designs, and symbols):

  • Copyright: Protects original literary works, artistic creations, and computer software code against unauthorized copying.
  • Patent: Exclusive rights granted for an invention that provides a novel technological solution or process.
  • Trademark: Symbols, logos, names, or brand slogans used to distinguish goods or services in the marketplace.
  • Software Piracy: The illegal copying, distribution, or commercial sale of copyrighted software without a proper license.

7. Cyber Law and Electronic Transaction Act (ETA) of Nepal

Cyber Law is the body of legal regulations governing the internet, digital communication, hardware usage, and electronic transactions.

In Nepal, the primary legal framework regulating digital activities is the Electronic Transaction Act (ETA), 2063 B.S. (2006 A.D.).

Key Objectives of ETA 2063:

  • To make electronic transactions authentic, secure, and legally recognized.
  • To govern and regulate the usage of Digital Signatures in official and commercial records.
  • To prevent and punish cybercrimes committed against computer systems, networks, and data in Nepal.
  • To establish a dedicated IT Tribunal and IT Appellate Tribunal for handling legal cases related to cyber offenses.
